Existing counter-UAV systems address only parts of this decision problem, and none of these approaches extends directly to the full problem. Rule-based and optimization-driven methods allocate jamming resources according to predefined threat priorities [hao2024]. Such priorities must be specified before deployment, yet threat severity is not directly measurable: it emerges from the interplay of distance, velocity, heading, RF activity, and temporal behaviors such as loitering or deceleration during an approach. Moreover, solving the allocation from the current snapshot alone ignores how each target's threat will evolve in the subsequent time slots. Learning-based methods can extract such patterns from data, but they typically optimize individual actions, such as communication-link selection or transmit-power control [rao2022]. Extending a single policy to select K jamming targets among Nt UAVs yields a joint action space of C(Nt, K) combinations, whose size grows combinatorially with Nt. Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) can coordinate multiple decisions [valianti2024, wang2025], but existing approaches generally assume a fixed number of UAVs and encode their states and actions using fixed-dimensional representations. 1.2 第三段：為什麼 RMAB 適合這個問題 main.tex:109

原文

By exploiting the RMAB structure, WhittleFormer decomposes target selection into per-UAV evaluations and ranks UAVs using their learned indices, thereby avoiding combinatorial search over joint actions. Its Transformer-based encoder … （其後未改動）

修改後

To address this challenge, we formulate counter-UAV jamming as a restless multi-armed bandit (RMAB) problem, which naturally matches its structure: each UAV is an arm whose state keeps evolving whether or not it is jammed, jamming a UAV corresponds to activating its arm, and limited jamming resources allow at most K arms to be activated per time slot. Although optimal RMAB control is PSPACE-hard [papadimitriou1999], the Whittle relaxation [whittle1988] decouples the problem into per-UAV subproblems, each summarized by a scalar Whittle index that quantifies the long-term benefit of jamming that UAV now. Jamming the K UAVs with the highest indices replaces a search over all joint actions with per-UAV evaluation and sorting, and this index policy is asymptotically optimal under suitable conditions [weber1990]. Because each index depends only on its own UAV, the same policy applies regardless of how many UAVs are present. We propose WhittleFormer, a scalable neural index policy that learns these indices directly from interaction data without requiring a known transition model. Its Transformer-based encoder … （其後未改動）

用詞上的取捨：
  • 降低複雜度的是 Whittle relaxation（拆解成逐架子問題），不是「建模成 RMAB」本身；PSPACE-hard 指的是一般 RMAB 的最優解。
  • 「asymptotically optimal」依賴 indexability，目前寫成 “under suitable conditions”；依 Part 2 的 2.1，indexability 可在訓練設定下直接證明。
  • Whittle index ≠ threat：index 衡量「現在 jam 它多換到的長期效益」，threat 只是當下危險程度。這個區別正好回答 R2-W1「為何用 Whittle index 當 priority」。
